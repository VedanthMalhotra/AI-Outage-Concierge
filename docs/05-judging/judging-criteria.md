# HackOHI/O judging criteria

Source: `OHIO_Judging_Rubric.xlsx`, sheet **"Judging Rubric"** (the final scoring sheet; the other sheets are drafts of the same rubric).

Six criteria in three categories, each scored **1 to 5 points**, for **30 points total**.

| Points | Level |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Accomplished |
| 4 | Advanced |
| 3 | Intermediate |
| 2 | Beginning |
| 1 | Developing |

## Summary

| Category | Criterion | What earns 5 points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity | Innovation | Researched and analyzed existing solutions (strengths and weaknesses), then explored alternatives that make a more successful solution |
| Curiosity | Curiosity | Asked new or unique questions as a team, made a plan to answer them, **and acted on that plan** |
| Connections | Communication | A **functional prototype** of the essential elements, plus sketches, diagrams and a presentation |
| Connections | Project breadth and complexity | Synthesized ideas from a wide variety of sources across humanities, science and engineering into something new, future-oriented and complex |
| Creating Value | Impact | A detailed concept that is **justified** (research, user needs) **and feasible** to develop |
| Creating Value | Navigating challenges | Reflected on failures and pivoted to alternatives that produced a more successful solution |

## Curiosity

> A curious disposition opens doors to continuous learning, new perspectives, and boundless growth opportunities. Curiosity often manifests in hackathon projects through innovative problem-solving approaches, unconventional ideas, diverse technologies, and a relentless pursuit of creative solutions to complex challenges.

### 1. Innovation

*Challenge the norm by analyzing existing solutions through testing or research, then exploring ways to improve or reinvent.*

- **Learning objective:** Analyze accepted solutions. Explore a contrarian view of currently accepted products, processes and services.
- **What it might look like:** A project that improves an existing solution in a way that increases value (access, affordability, opportunity, empowerment), or a brand-new approach to a problem. Both involve identifying a problem and examining existing solutions.
- **Judges ask:** *How did your team challenge commonly accepted ideas, and what did you do differently in your project?*

| Pts | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Based the project on research, analyzed accepted solutions for strengths or weaknesses, **and explored one or more new or alternative solutions**. |
| 4 | Researched and analyzed existing solutions (strengths and weaknesses) but did not explore alternatives. |
| 3 | Researched existing solutions for context but did not analyze strengths and weaknesses. |
| 2 | No research or analysis; worked within an already known or given area of improvement. |
| 1 | No research or analysis, and no improvement identified. |

### 2. Curiosity

*Being curious, ask questions that inspire growth and result in the artful application of scientific principles.*

- **Learning objective:** Demonstrate curiosity. Ask and encourage questions that facilitate and inspire growth and learning.
- **What it might look like:** A team that questioned existing limitations, sought out an unaddressed problem, or built something outside the box with unique ideas and processes.
- **Judges ask:** *Can you share examples of questions your team asked while exploring possibilities? Show us something unique, beautiful or clever in your project.*

| Pts | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Identified knowledge gaps together, asked new or unique questions, made a plan, **and acted on it** to improve project outcomes. |
| 4 | Identified gaps, asked new or unique questions, and made a plan to explore answers. |
| 3 | Identified gaps and asked relevant new or unique questions as a team. |
| 2 | Asked some questions to understand the area of interest. |
| 1 | Asked no questions. |

## Connections

> Connecting diverse sets of information and skills unlocks the potential for groundbreaking innovation by fostering the cross-pollination of ideas and the synthesis of novel solutions.

### 3. Communication

*Effectively communicating project concepts, including a visual representation that explains the project's development process and purpose.*

- **Learning objective:** Develop concepts and visual representations. Represent and refine conceptual solutions through visuals.
- **What it might look like:** The project video or slide deck, plus what is shown in the interview: slides, a physical or software prototype, a poster or a video describing the project and process.
- **Judges ask:** *What part of your presentation shows off your work best? Can you show me your prototype and how it explains your concept?*

| Pts | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | **Functional prototype** of the essential elements, with sketches, drawings and diagrams. |
| 4 | Prototype of the essential elements plus a detailed presentation. |
| 3 | Detailed sketches or diagrams of essential elements, **or** a prototype without detailed documentation. |
| 2 | Basic sketches or diagrams of some elements. |
| 1 | No prototype or representation. |

### 4. Project breadth and complexity

*Leveraging knowledge and skills from the humanities, engineering and science to make complex, nontrivial innovations.*

- **Learning objective:** Integrate information by making connections between different domains of knowledge.
- **What it might look like:** Diverse frameworks, APIs, data or perspectives, and programming best practices. Resources from different domains (for example Medicine + Robotics, Environmental Science + Economics). Consideration of future growth, scalability and sustainability.
- **Judges ask:** *How did you use information from two different areas to explore a new idea? What was the most complicated piece to build? What changes if this project is scaled to 10x the size or 10x the people?*

| Pts | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Synthesized ideas from a wide variety of sources across humanities, science and engineering into something new, future-oriented and effectively complex. |
| 4 | Ideas from more than one knowledge area, **including at least one area not directly related to computing**. |
| 3 | Ideas from more than one technical knowledge area. |
| 2 | Ideas from a single technical knowledge area. |
| 1 | A single idea from a single knowledge area. |

## Creating Value

> Opportunities often lie in the intersection of failure and persistence. We look for projects that demonstrate adaptability and new products, processes or services that provide potential social, economic or environmental value. Does this project address the right problem? Does it consider what stakeholders hold most valuable, and has the team applied lessons learned from a setback or mistake?

### 5. Impact

*The project seeks to provide a solution with potential social, economic or business, environmental or other tangible value.*

- **Learning objective:** Identify an opportunity to create value.
- **What it might look like:** Well-defined user needs, or a project that considers the reach, adoption and long-term effects of its implementation.
- **Judges ask:** *What does your project improve, or who might it help? What user needs did you keep in mind? Did you get feedback from your target audience?*

| Pts | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Detailed concept (solution, prototype) that is **justified and feasible** to develop, with social, economic or environmental value. |
| 4 | Detailed, justified concept with a clear value proposition, but not feasible to develop. |
| 3 | Features identified **and refined using research**. |
| 2 | Features identified. |
| 1 | No features identified. |

### 6. Navigating challenges

*Testing ideas, recognizing barriers or failures as important moments of feedback, and pivoting accordingly.*

- **Learning objective:** Learn from failure. Persist through and learn from failure.
- **What it might look like:** A crucial pivot because of a knowledge gap, or changes made after testing revealed a problem.
- **Judges ask:** *Name one thing you struggled with or that didn't work at all, and how you pivoted. How did you test your ideas and change things based on the results?*

| Pts | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Reflected on failures and shifted approach, so the alternatives produced **a more successful solution**. |
| 4 | Identified failures, reflected on alternatives, and implemented them. |
| 3 | Identified failures and considered alternative approaches. |
| 2 | Identified a failure or area of improvement. |
| 1 | Identified nothing. |

## How this project should use the rubric

A checklist for every piece of work on the AI Outage Concierge:

- [ ] **Innovation:** keep a written analysis of existing outage tools (utility outage maps, ETR systems, alert programs, academic models) with strengths, weaknesses and how we differ. See [existing-solutions.md](../03-research/existing-solutions.md).
- [ ] **Curiosity:** log the questions the team asked, the plan to answer each, and what we did. Show one clever or unique feature in the demo.
- [ ] **Communication:** ship a working prototype of the full customer journey, plus an architecture diagram and a slide deck or video.
- [ ] **Breadth and complexity:** name the domains combined (power engineering, geospatial/GIS, graph topology, ML/statistics, LLMs, crisis communication and human factors, accessibility, privacy and ethics). Have a "10x scale" answer ready.
- [ ] **Impact:** state the user needs, cite research on the cost and harm of outages and poor communication, and show the design is feasible for AEP to adopt.
- [ ] **Navigating challenges:** keep a running log of what failed, what we learned and how we pivoted (for example data gaps, model accuracy, joins that didn't work).
